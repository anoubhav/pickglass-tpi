How to annotate — one dot per yarn the guide line crosses

Protocol. The 150 files are only 76 distinct photos (scenes): the other files are copies (byte-identical, a quarter-resolution copy, a PNG copy or a synthetic crop). Annotate one photo per scene (Show → Scenes to annotate, the default); a copy shows a teal badge “same scene as #… — labels copied”, is read-only, and G opens its scene. Annotate the 20 IAA subset scenes first (Show → IAA subset, purple badge). They open in blind mode (no machine proposals): count from scratch and never turn blind off on them; every toggle is logged. Then do the rest (Show → Scenes to annotate, or N) with proposals, which are often wrong (one dot per stripe or per float pair). In a two-colour plain weave a light and the next dark float are two yarns. If glare hides yarns, move the line (drag its round handle) to a clearer row.

Check carefully. Some photos show an orange Check carefully: … or a yellow Take care: … badge with a reason, e.g. the automatic estimators disagree on a line, the yarns are fine for the resolution (zoom in), glare or blur. It comes from machine checks only, never shows a count and says nothing about which count is right: count every yarn as usual. It never appears on IAA-subset or blind images. Show → High attention first lists the other photos with the flagged ones first (← → and N follow that order).

Turning the image. , . turn the image −/+0.5° (Shift 5°), the Image slider sets any angle up to ±45°, and / turns it back to 0°. The guide lines stay where they are on screen: the fabric turns under them, so you can turn it until the yarns cross the lines at right angles. On the fabric the lines turn by the same angle the other way (their angles in the card change), so the count is exactly the count of turned lines: dots ÷ line length inside the window (the turned dashed square). R (Align image) turns the image until the selected line crosses its yarns at right angles, using the same estimate as A; A turns the line instead. [ ] and 0 still turn a single line on the fabric (0° = parallel to the window edge). Zoom, pan, clicks, box-delete and handles all work on the turned image.

Work autosaves in this browser and, with python3 server.py, to data/saved/. Use Download (JSON + CSV) at the end of every session; Import restores a download (newer record wins). The timer counts active time only: it pauses after 60 s without input and an idle gap longer than 60 s is not counted.

Examples

  • One dot on every yarn the guide line crosses, edge to edge; an edge yarn counts if its centre is inside the window.
  • Count yarns, not colour blocks or twill diagonals: in a two-colour plain weave a light and the next dark float are two yarns.
  • The machine dots are often wrong (one per stripe or per float pair): check every one, add missed yarns, delete extras.
  • Tilted yarns: A turns the line across its yarns, or , . / R turn the image; TPI = dots ÷ line length in the window.
  • Glare or blur: drag the line’s round handle to a clearer row, zoom until yarns are ~10 px apart, E for the enhanced view.
  • IAA subset (purple badge): count blind from scratch and never turn blind off. No example below is from the IAA subset or shows the same patch of fabric as an IAA scene.
